Those numbers are the count of steps in each SMAART Framework. They’re there for two practical reasons:
to keep the framework simple enough to remember and apply, and
to make it auditable and assessable (each step can be taught, practiced, and tested).
So:
CLEAR-5 = a decision tool with 5 steps
REGULATE-4 = a self-management tool with 4 steps
SIGNAL-3 = a communication tool with 3 steps, etc.
Below is the detailed meaning of each framework and its steps.

1) CLEAR-5 (Decision Quality)
Used for: CRQ-led skills like critical thinking, problem structuring, trade-offs, judgement.
The 5 steps
C — Clarify the decision
What exactly must be decided? (not the problem, the decision)
L — List constraints
Time, resources, rules, stakeholders, dependencies.
E — Evaluate risks & trade-offs
What can go wrong? What is acceptable vs unacceptable?
A — Act on the most defensible option
Choose the option you can justify under constraints.
R — Record assumptions & rationale
Document what you assumed, why you chose it, and what to watch.
Why 5 steps: enough to be rigorous, short enough to be usable in real work and interviews.

2) REGULATE-4 (Self-Management)
Used for: SRQ-led skills like focus, discipline, stress management, resilience.
The 4 steps
Recognise
Notice what’s happening: distraction, stress, procrastination, emotional escalation.
Reset
Quick physiological/behavioural reset: pause, breathe, remove distraction, time-box.
Reframe
Shift thinking: “What’s the next controllable action?” “What’s the priority?”
Repeat
Build consistency: routines, check-ins, accountability triggers.
Why 4 steps: self-management needs speed; too many steps fail under pressure.

3) LOOP-4 (Learning Agility)
Used for: LQ-led skills like upskilling, feedback use, adaptation, learning from failure.
The 4 steps
Learn
Identify what you need, find the best source, set a short learning goal.
Try
Apply immediately in a small task or prototype.
Observe
Check outcome: feedback, errors, results, gaps.
Polish
Improve and repeat the loop with a tighter approach.
Why 4 steps: learning agility is cyclic; this makes it repeatable and measurable.

4) SIGNAL-3 (Communication & Influence)
Used for: SIQ-led skills like stakeholder management, collaboration, conflict navigation.
The 3 steps
Set context
What are we trying to achieve? What matters? What constraints exist?
Inform clearly
State your point with clarity (facts, options, recommendation).
Gain alignment
Confirm understanding, next steps, owners, deadlines.
Why 3 steps: communication must be memorable and usable in live meetings.

5) EXEC-5 (Execution Discipline)
Used for: PEQ-led skills like delivery, quality, reliability, ownership.
The 5 steps
Define scope & outcome
What “done” means and what success looks like.
Plan the path
Break into tasks, dependencies, timeline, risk points.
Execute with focus
Do the work; protect deep-work blocks; manage interruptions.
Verify quality
Test, review, validate against requirements.
Close & communicate
Handover, document, report status, capture learnings.
Why 5 steps: execution requires a full loop from scope to closure, not just “doing work.”

6) GUARD-4 (AI / Digital Responsibility)
Used for: DAQ-led skills like AI literacy, data safety, verification of outputs.
The 4 steps
Ground
What is the task? What data is allowed? What context is missing?
Assess risk
Security, privacy, bias, compliance, reputational risks.
Review output
Verify accuracy, check sources, test if needed.
Decide & log
Make a decision and record what tool was used and what was verified.
Why 4 steps: responsible digital use must be fast, consistent, and auditable.

7) ETHOS-4 (Ethics & Sustainability Judgement)
Used for: SEQ-led skills like integrity, fairness, long-term impact.
The 4 steps
Stakeholders
Who is affected now and later?
Harm/Benefit
What harms could occur? What benefits are real?
Duties/Rules
What obligations, laws, policies, or professional duties apply?
Transparent choice
Choose the most responsible option and be transparent about trade-offs.
Why 4 steps: ethics must be structured but practical; too many steps paralyse decisions.
