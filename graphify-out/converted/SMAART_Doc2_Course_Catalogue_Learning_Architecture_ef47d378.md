# Section 1 — The Architecture at a Glance
The SMAART programme is organised in six concentric layers. A student sees the top layer (dashboard). An institution sees the middle layers (cohort reporting). A regulator or an employer audits from the bottom up. Every layer feeds the one above it. This catalogue describes the FULL content available at every tier (Tier 1 SMAART Core, Tier 2 SMAART Standard, Tier 3 SMAART Complete); the differences between tiers are which TRACKS are included versus available as add-ons, plus the depth of support and renewal. All 25 HI courses are present on every tier. See Document 1B for the complete tier-by-tier mapping.

# Section 2 — The Three Stages of the Core Programme
The 25 Human Intelligence courses are delivered in three stages that match the developmental sequence of a professional career. A student cannot work with others well (Capability) without first managing themselves (Capacity); cannot lead others well (Leadership) without first working effectively alongside them (Capability). The stages are sequential and gated.
## 2.1 Stage 1 — CAPACITY: 'Can I manage myself and think clearly?'
- When: Year 1 UG / Semester 1 PG (Track B for postgraduates).
- Domain emphasis: THINKING + ADAPTING.
- Quotients developed: CRQ, SRQ, PEQ (and foundational SIQ via networking).
- Number of courses: 10 courses (S01–S10) including 6 delivered in Year 1 and 4 in Year 2.
- Gate to Stage 2: 8 of 10 Capacity courses passed at ≥70% + T2 stage assessment taken.
Why it comes first: McKinsey's 18,000-person DELTAs study found that Self-Leadership is the single strongest predictor of employment — stronger than any cognitive or interpersonal skill. A student who cannot think clearly or manage themselves is a liability in any team regardless of technical strength. Capacity is the foundation.
## 2.2 Stage 2 — CAPABILITY: 'Can I work with others and deliver results?'
- When: Year 2–3 UG / Semester 2–3 PG.
- Domain emphasis: CONNECTING + THINKING applied to interpersonal contexts.
- Quotients developed: SIQ (primary), DAQ.
- Number of courses: 9 courses (S11–S19) including S15 GUARD-4, the AIQ Track gateway.
- Gate to Stage 3: 7 of 9 Capability courses passed at ≥70% + T3 stage assessment taken.
Why it comes second: WEF ranks Leadership and Social Influence as the #3 global core skill. NASSCOM's annual industry feedback consistently identifies communication as the #1 gap in Indian graduates. These capabilities assume Capacity is already in place — a student cannot communicate clearly if they cannot think clearly.
## 2.3 Stage 3 — LEADERSHIP: 'Can I influence, create and lead?'
- When: Year 3–4 UG / Semester 3–4 PG.
- Domain emphasis: CREATING + CONNECTING applied to organisational contexts.
- Quotients developed: LQ, SEQ.
- Number of courses: 6 courses (S19–S24) including S21 LENS-4, the SQ Track gateway.
- Programme completion: 5 of 6 Leadership courses passed + T4 stage assessment taken.
Why it comes last: McKinsey projects 19% growth in demand for higher cognitive skills (creativity, innovation, systems thinking) by 2030. WEF lists creative thinking as both the #4 core skill and the #3 fastest-growing skill. Leadership is not just for managers; it is required in any role where initiative and ethical judgment matter. It rests on the other two stages.

# Section 3 — The 25 Human Intelligence Courses
Each course is a 25–35-minute module that teaches one named framework, produces one skill, and updates one quotient. The Year 1 sequencing (6 courses) is specified in Document 27 of the core spec; the full 25-course sequence spans 2–4 years and is specified in Document 28.
## 3.1 Capacity stage (10 courses: S01–S10)
Self-management and structured thinking. Every student starts here.

## 3.2 Capability stage (9 courses: S11–S19)
Working with others: communication, collaboration, AI fluency, systems thinking. S15 GUARD-4 unlocks the AIQ Track.

## 3.3 Leadership stage (6 courses: S19–S24)
Influence, ethics, creativity, delegation, change. S21 LENS-4 unlocks the SQ Track. (Note: S19 Negotiation appears in both stages because it bridges Capability and Leadership — counted once in progression.)

# Section 4 — The Three Readiness Tracks
Each track is an optional, stackable specialist certification that runs alongside the core programme. All three use the same architecture: 5 courses, 5 competencies, 4 assessment methods. Each track takes roughly 3 hours to complete and is valid for 12 months with an annual 15-minute renewal. Certification is single-level: a student is either 'SMAART [Track] Certified' (all 5 course scores ≥70% AND capstone ≥65%) or '[Track] In Progress'. See Document 1B Section 11.2 for the certification threshold rule. See Document 1B for which tier includes which track.
## 4.1 PIQ — Personal Intelligence Track
- Purpose: Build the self-awareness, emotional regulation, growth mindset, purpose and confidence that make a professional effective under pressure.
- Prerequisite: S05 (RESET-4) complete — the foundation course on adaptive recovery.
- When available: From Year 1, earliest of all three tracks. Feeds the PEQ quotient.

## 4.2 AIQ — AI Readiness Track
- Purpose: Not how to USE AI tools. How to WORK WITH AI as a colleague. Orchestrate, judge, split, govern, evolve.
- Prerequisite: S15 GUARD-4 + any one external AI literacy course (Anthropic AI Fluency for Students is free; Google AI Professional Certificate and Microsoft AB-730 are accepted).
- When available: Capability stage (Year 2 onwards). Feeds the DAQ quotient.

## 4.3 SQ — Sustainability Readiness Track
- Purpose: Not how to be an environmentalist. How to make sustainable decisions in any job, any sector.
- Prerequisite: S21 LENS-4 (Ethical Judgment). Recommended: S16 ZOOM-3 (Systems Thinking). No external course required.
- When available: Leadership stage (Year 3 onwards). Feeds the SEQ quotient.

# Section 5 — Progression Gates, Prerequisites and Semester Logic
The progression architecture is strictly sequential and strictly calendar-aligned. Courses unlock one at a time within a semester; semesters unlock only when the calendar says so; stages unlock only when ALL courses of the prior stage have been passed at the 70% threshold. This rigour is deliberate. The Skills Passport and the SRI carry trust only if the gate that preceded them is known to be robust. A single exception path — the Fast Learner Approval Workflow (Section 5.5) — exists for students who have demonstrably outperformed the expected pace, subject to documented institutional sign-off.
## 5.1 Stage gates
To progress from one stage to the next, the student must have PASSED every course at the prior stage at ≥70%, AND taken the relevant stage assessment. No partial completion. No 'most of them' heuristic. ALL means ALL.

## 5.3 Within-stage sequencing — sequential unlock
Inside a semester, courses unlock one at a time in a published order. Course N+1 becomes available only when Course N has been PASSED at ≥70%. A student cannot see the next course before passing the current course. The rationale is pedagogical: each course teaches a framework that the next course assumes. Allowing a student to attempt them out of order degrades the outcome and devalues the SRI for the whole cohort.
- Semester 1 (Year 1): S01 CLEAR-5 → S07 OWN-4 → S05 RESET-4. At the start of the semester only S01 is visible. S07 appears on the dashboard once S01 is passed. S05 appears once S07 is passed. PIQ Track unlocks once S05 is passed.
- Semester 2 (Year 1): S03 READ-3 → S09 VALUE-4 → S10 LINK-4. Same sequential unlock.
- Capability stage: S11 → S12 → S13 → S14 → S15 → S16 → S17 → S18 → S19. S15 GUARD-4 is the AIQ gateway; AIQ Track courses begin their own sequential unlock after S15 plus the external AI prerequisite.
- Leadership stage: S20 EDGE-4 → S21 LENS-4 → S22 GROW-4 → S23 HAND-4 → S24 MOVE-4 → S25 FLIP-3. S21 is the SQ gateway; SQ Track begins sequential unlock after S21.
- Tracks (PIQ, AIQ, SQ): Each track's 5 courses also unlock sequentially (PIQ-1 → PIQ-2 → … → PIQ-5, and similarly for AIQ and SQ). The track's capstone trade-off is available only after all 5 courses are passed.

## 5.4 Between-semester sequencing — calendar-locked
Semester N+1 courses are NOT available until the calendar Semester N+1 begins, regardless of how fast the student completes Semester N. A student who finishes the three Semester 1 courses in six weeks does not see Semester 2 courses in week seven. They wait for the institution's Semester 2 start date.
This is a deliberate design choice, not a platform limitation. Two reasons:
- Retention. Ebbinghaus's forgetting curve (Document 1 Section 2.2.5) shows that learners lose approximately 50% of new material within a week of acquisition. Spacing acquisition across the semester window, with daily micro-challenges and monthly fitness checks filling the gap, produces measurably better retention at T2. A student who binges Semester 1 in six weeks loses the early frameworks before Semester 2 begins.
- Institutional alignment. Colleges operate on academic calendars. Placement cycles, internship windows, exam weeks and convocation schedules are all calendar-bound. SMAART's semester lock keeps the SMAART journey aligned with the institutional rhythm and makes the Placement Officer dashboard meaningful (cohort-level views rely on the cohort being at a comparable stage).


## 5.5 Fast Learner Exception — Approval Workflow
A student who has demonstrably exceeded the expected pace may request early unlock of the next semester's courses through a formal approval workflow. This is an exception, not a default. It is not available on demand and is not granted automatically based on completion speed alone. It exists because, in rare cases, a genuinely exceptional student is better served by moving ahead than by waiting — and the institutional sign-off protects both the student and the integrity of the SRI.
### 5.5.1 Eligibility — all four must hold
- The student has PASSED all courses of the current semester at ≥80% (higher than the 70% gate — evidence of genuine strength, not minimum compliance).
- The student's latest stage assessment (T1 / T2 / T3) overall score is ≥70% ('Proficient' band).
- Framework Retention (from T2 onwards) is ≥85%.
- The student has no open flags: no appeals pending, no anti-gaming flags raised, no remediation plan active.

### 5.5.3 Safeguards on the Fast Learner path
- Acceleration is a ONE-TIME grant per semester. A student who is accelerated from Semester 1 to Semester 2 must apply again (and re-qualify) to accelerate from Semester 2 to Semester 3.
- Acceleration unlocks the NEXT semester only — not multiple semesters ahead. A student cannot leap from Semester 1 to Semester 3 in one request.
- Sequential unlock within the new semester still applies. The student sees S03 only on entering Semester 2, and sees S09 only after passing S03.
- The Skills Passport does NOT record or display acceleration status. Employers see the same standards, scores and SRI they would see for any other student. Acceleration is a delivery mechanic, not a credential.
- Quarterly bias audit (Document 2A Section B.4.2) checks whether acceleration is being granted disproportionately to any demographic group. If a pattern emerges, the eligibility thresholds are reviewed.
### 5.5.4 Worked example
Anjali is in Year 1 Semester 1 of a B.Com programme. She completes S01 CLEAR-5 in Week 3 at 82%, S07 OWN-4 in Week 5 at 85%, and S05 RESET-4 in Week 7 at 88%. She has maintained daily micro-challenges throughout. Semester 2 is scheduled to begin in Week 18. In Week 8 she submits a Request Accelerated Progression form. The system checks: all courses passed at ≥80% (yes), no T-assessment yet (T1 score was 68 — does not meet the ≥70% rule, but T1 alone does not disqualify a Semester 1 → Semester 2 acceleration request because T1 is a baseline, not a progress measure — the rule applies from T2 onwards). The PO reviews Anjali's behavioural pattern, confirms consistency, endorses. The Programme Head confirms her attendance and group-project participation are on track. Anjali confirms opt-in. Semester 2 courses unlock in Week 9 — nine weeks ahead of schedule. She completes Semester 2 by Week 16, then immediately takes T2 at the normal T2 trigger (5+ courses passed, 4 months since T1 — now met). Her SRI is calculated on exactly the same formula. She is not advantaged or disadvantaged in scoring — only in time.

# Section 7 — Why Sector-Agnostic and Role-Agnostic is the Correct Design
A common question from an institution or an employer is: 'Do you have a version of the course for banking / healthcare / engineering?' The answer is: the SKILL is universal; the SCENARIO contextualises it. Here is why that is right.
## 7.1 The same framework works identically across sectors
CLEAR-5 (Clarify, List constraints, Evaluate, Act, Record) applies identically to a finance analyst choosing a valuation method, a doctor selecting a treatment plan, a software developer picking an architecture, a teacher designing a lesson, and a retail manager setting a pricing decision. The STRUCTURE of the capability is universal. The CONTENT changes. A CLEAR-5-trained graduate carries the capability into any of those settings; a graduate trained in 'banking decision-making' has no transferable capability if they change sector.
## 7.2 Sector variation lives in the scenarios and in technical skills
Within the SJT pool (604 scenarios), the situations are drawn from multiple sectors: a software team, a hospital ward, a law firm, a retail shop floor, a manufacturing plant, a marketing agency, a school. Character names cross cultures and sectors: Priya, James, Fatima, Wei, Adaeze, Rohan, Elena. No single sector or culture dominates. This produces assessment validity across every sector the graduate might enter.
Technical skill verification, in contrast, IS sector-specific (Document 21 of the core spec). A commerce student verifies Tally ERP, GST basics, financial accounting. A computer science student verifies Python, SQL and Git. A nursing student verifies SOAP notes and clinical documentation. The technical layer is specific; the behavioural layer is universal. The SRI combines both.
## 7.3 The benefit to the learner
A graduate who changes sector — which McKinsey estimates most graduates will do 3–5 times in a career — does not lose the 10 Standards, the 7 Quotients, or the SRI. Those travel with them. The technical skills get added to over a career. The foundation is portable. This is the opposite of how a vocational certificate works, and it is precisely why SMAART survives the WEF finding that 39% of today's skills will be obsolete by 2030.
## 7.4 The benefit to the employer
An employer hiring across sectors (most large corporates, all placement agencies, all consulting firms, all Big Four auditors) can compare candidates from different degrees on the same scale. A B.Com student from a Tier 3 college and a B.Tech student from a Tier 1 IIT can be compared side-by-side on STRUCTURE, COMMUNICATE, CONNECT, VERIFY and the 7 quotients. The employer can then overlay their specific technical requirements. This is the fairness mechanism the market has lacked.
## 7.5 The benefit to the institution
A single platform serves every department. The commerce faculty, the engineering faculty, the arts faculty and the law faculty all use the same SMAART courses, the same SRI, the same Skills Passport. Cohort-level comparisons become meaningful. IQAC reporting becomes consistent. Placement strategy becomes sharper. The institution's investment is amortised across all programmes, not fragmented by discipline.

| What happens when a course cannot reach 70%
A student who has attempted a course 3 times without reaching 70% enters Targeted Remediation. The Placement Officer configures a support plan (mentor pairing, additional practice scenarios, tutoring), a 30-day cooling period applies, and the student retakes with a fresh SJT variant set. The course stays UNPASSED and the stage gate remains locked until 70% is reached. This preserves the rigour of the gate while respecting the never-permanently-stuck principle (see Document 2A Section B.1.1). A student never loses the right to progress; the timing of that progression is shaped by demonstrated capability, not by calendar pressure. |

| What the student sees before the next semester opens
A student who has finished all Semester 1 courses early sees a 'Semester 1 complete' dashboard with the date Semester 2 opens. In the intervening period, daily micro-challenges continue (feeding the Learning Consistency category of the SRI), the PIQ Track remains available if unlocked, the English module continues, and the student can begin technical skill verification. There is no idle period — the calendar lock applies only to the next semester's HI courses, not to the other layers of the programme. |

| Why this design is defensible to a regulator
The acceleration exception is documented, has explicit criteria, requires dual institutional sign-off, is auditable, and is quarterly-audited for demographic disparity. A student who wants to accelerate has a clear path; a student who does not want to accelerate faces zero pressure. The default is semester-locked rigour; the exception is principled and narrow. This is the same pattern as credit portability in NEP 2020 and the UK apprenticeship fast-track — structured flexibility, not informal permission. |
