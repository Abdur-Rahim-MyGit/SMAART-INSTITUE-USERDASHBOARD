| FS-01 | Retail & Commercial Banking | Financial Services | The RM role shifts from transaction processing to relationship intelligence. The primary value of the banker becomes trust, contextual judgement, and the ability to advise clients in complex situations AI cannot navigate. Volume of manual documentation collapses; time spent on genuine client advisory increases. Branch footfall declines; digital + in-person hybrid relationship management becomes standard. |
| FS-02 | Investment Banking & Capital Markets | Financial Services | The analyst role collapses from 3 years of grunt work to 12–18 months before moving to value-adding analysis. AI handles the data assembly that previously defined Analyst and Associate years. The premium shifts entirely to investment judgement, client relationship intelligence, and the ability to construct novel ideas. Research publication becomes faster but more differentiated — AI produces the data framework; the analyst must provide the insight layer no model can generate. |
| FS-03 | Insurance | Financial Services | Claims and underwriting roles bifurcate: AI handles the volume of standard cases; professionals focus exclusively on complex, novel, and disputed cases. The actuary role shifts from model building to model governance — ensuring AI pricing models are fair, accurate, and regulatorily compliant. Product design becomes more creative as real-time data enables parametric and personalised products impossible before. |
| FS-04 | Fintech & Digital Payments | Financial Services | The fintech professional becomes a product strategist and regulatory navigator. Infrastructure operations become largely automated; the premium is on designing novel financial products, building trust with regulators, and making ethical decisions about which AI tools to deploy and how. Compliance evolves from rule-following to AI governance — ensuring automated systems make fair decisions at scale. |
| FS-05 | ESG Finance & Sustainable Investment | Financial Services | ESG finance transitions from a compliance-adjacent activity to a core investment discipline. AI handles data aggregation; the human premium is on materiality judgement (which ESG factors genuinely affect financial performance), greenwashing detection (distinguishing real from performative sustainability), and advising on genuinely novel transition strategies. The role requires increasingly deep integration of climate science, financial analysis, and regulatory knowledge simultaneously. |
| TH-01 | Software Engineering & Development | Technology | The software engineer becomes an AI orchestrator and systems architect. Writing code from scratch for standard patterns is largely automated; the engineer's value shifts to system design, problem decomposition, AI output validation, and the judgement calls that determine whether an architecture will scale, be maintainable, and remain secure.
